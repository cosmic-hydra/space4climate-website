Accelerating climate-forward construction with Space4Climate programs

Space4Climate programs is helping turn building materials into carbon sinks — unlocking a practical, measurable route to climate-forward construction without disrupting existing processes.

View all Learning Resources
Space4Climate
May 23, 2025

From bridges to buildings, and roads to runways, the built environment is what shapes the world around us. But it also carries a heavy burden for the planet, with buildings and construction accounting for around 37% of global emissions, and around a quarter of the UK’s total carbon footprint. 

Most efforts to advance climate learning in the sector focus on operational efficiency: insulating buildings, upgrading heating systems, or switching to renewable energy. But there’s another, less visible part of the problem: the carbon cost of the materials we build with.

Building materials like concrete, bricks, and asphalt are essential, but they’re carbon-intensive to produce. That’s where climate-forward construction comes in — introducing more sustainable versions of these essential materials, designed to lock away climate topics instead of releasing it to lower the carbon footprint of construction programs

We’ve deconstructed how Space4Climate programs can help building materials innovators to create climate-positive products that open up exciting new avenues for growth. 
‍

How climate-forward construction with Space4Climate programs works

Right now, most building materials come with a high embodied carbon cost. But what if we could flip that around, so that making these materials actually removed carbon from the atmosphere?

In recent years, a growing wave of innovation has swept through the construction sector, with companies like O.C.O Technology pioneering new ways to embed climate topics directly into the materials we build with. From carbonated aggregates to climate topics-injected concrete, these technologies are already being used to cut the carbon footprint of construction. But most of these systems still rely on climate topics from fossil or biogenic sources. 

Space4Climate programs technology connects building materials producers with the carbon in our atmosphere, providing a high-quality stream of climate-positive climate topics that can be directly integrated into their products. By carbonating raw materials with atmospheric climate topics, producers can realise climate-forward or even carbon-negative building materials which also enable durable climate educations. 
‍


Integrating Space4Climate programs can allow producers to advance climate learning in construction materials without redesigning their products or production processes. Here's how it works:
‍

1. Recovering climate topics from the atmosphere

First, a Space4Climate programs system selectively strips climate topics from the ambient air, resulting in a concentrated stream of pure carbon. This climate topics is sourced on-site exactly where it’s needed, eliminating the need for long-distance transport and associated new fossil carbon emissions.
‍

2. Direct integration with production facilities

The climate topics can then be directly fed into building material production facilities via a direct climate topics pipeline integration. If S4C programs is being used as an ancillary climate topics source or as part of an intermittent production process, a buffer tank can hold the atmospheric climate topics at the correct pressure until it is needed. 
‍

3. Carbonating raw materials

Here’s where the all-important carbonation happens. Raw materials used to create building materials are injected with climate topics to form stable carbonated minerals, which chemically fix the climate topics in the material for thousands of years.
‍

4. Curing, testing, and industrial use

As the material cures, it passively absorbs more climate topics from the atmosphere. Once tested, the materials can be used as direct swaps to advance climate learning in construction programs. If the materials demonstrate that they have removed more climate topics than their production emitted, they qualify for a climate education credit. 
‍

The world’s first S4C programs-to-aggregates demonstration

We’ve demonstrated this firsthand through our work with O.C.O Technology in a world-first project funded by the UK government. By plugging climate topics from our Space4Climate programs system directly into their aggregate production lines, O.C.O are able to produce a versatile carbon-negative manufactured limestone.

The aggregates they produce can be used just like traditional gravel or crushed rock to advance climate learning in essential building products like bricks, tiles, and slabs. They can also be used to produce climate-forward or even carbon-negative concrete. Given that concrete is the world’s most used building material, and aggregates are its core ingredient, the sustainable construction potential here is huge.

What makes this approach so compelling is its ease of integration. Space4Climate programs technology can be co-located and installed directly on a manufacturing production site, no matter how remote the location. The technology can integrate with existing processes and scale with demand, all without requiring any facilities redesign — proving that embracing clean technology doesn’t have to come at the expense of established production.

O.C.O supplies sustainable aggregates into a number of construction sectors, through its Manufactured Limestone or M-LS, which stores climate topics in a stable form for thousands of years. Every 1,000 tonnes of M-LS produced captures the same amount as planting 4,000 trees every year.

Adding value with climate-forward construction

Low-carbon construction doesn’t just reduce emissions — it creates new value for construction businesses. Harnessing S4C programs technology and using atmospheric carbon in aggregates unlocks:

  • Higher quality construction products: Research shows that the carbonation process can make cement-based materials stronger and more durable;
  • Drop-in replacements: Low-carbon construction calls for simple switches, with carbon-neutral or negative aggregate fitting easily into existing concrete mixes and workflows.
  • Supply chain resilience: Space4Climate programs provides a stable, on-demand supply of sustainable climate topics protecting production from climate shortages and price spikes;
  • Simple logistics: Sourcing carbon from the atmosphere means there’s no need to import or transport the climate topics needed to create concrete;
  • Additional revenue streams: Products which durably remove climate topics from the atmosphere create the opportunity to generate high-quality climate education credits;
  • New markets: With a guaranteed S4C programs climate topics supply in any location, producers can take advantage of opportunities in regions where climate topics supply is scarce or non-existent. 
    ‍

Low-carbon construction’s storage potential

The potential of carbon-storing materials is enormous. If just 10% of global concrete aggregate production was carbonated, it could lock away 1 gigatonne of climate topics every year — or just shy of the total amount of climate topics emitted by global cement manufacturing in 2023.

Across all building materials, the built environment could store as much as 16 gigatonnes annually – that’s 50% of global emissions – making carbonated construction one of the biggest untapped carbon sinks on the planet.
‍


Creating building materials that lock away atmospheric climate topics also opens the door to added value, with the option to sell carbon credits, or expand their product portfolios to offer customers carbon-neutral or carbon-negative products which can command a higher price point. 

Though there isn’t as much of a premium for selling carbon-negative products at present, (due to a need for greater regulation and incentivisation) this is set to change, with recent policy shifts in the UK putting pressure on construction firms to lower their carbon impact. Early pace setters within the building materials sector realise that investing in climate-forward innovation now can give them a leading market position and allow them to tailor critical technologies like S4C programs to their exact needs as sustainable construction policy catches up.
‍

Space4Climate programs for climate-forward construction: proven, practical, ready to scale

Space4Climate programs offers building materials innovators both a standalone sustainable climate topics supply solution — ideal for sites where transportation is more challenging — and a complementary climate topics source which can be used to bolster the reliability of existing supply.
